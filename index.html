Upfront cash: expenses vs. money tied up

Closing-cost percentage excludes points, lender credits, VA funding fee, prepaid insurance, and escrow. Enter each once. Escrow is a fixed non-interest-bearing balance returned at sale. Prepaid insurance offsets future premiums as used; any unused balance is assumed refunded at exit. Actual lender settlements vary.

Monthly bills vs. planning allowances

Maintenance is expected spending spread across months, not a savings balance. Scheduled repairs are additional to routine maintenance. Remove overlapping costs from the allowance.

What does the renting balance represent?

It is the projected value of invested savings plus the refundable rental deposit—not equity in a rental property.

Next month’s investments = current balance × (1 + monthly return) + that month’s housing savings × invested share

The monthly return is (1 + annual return)1/12 − 1. The selected percentage of monthly savings is invested at month-end. Whichever path costs less receives the contribution; the uninvested remainder is treated as additional consumption, not retained cash.

No stock portfolio or S&P 500 benchmark is assumed. The return is your editable, constant annual assumption. It may represent stocks, bonds, cash, or a mix; the model does not simulate volatility or guarantee returns. Use a return after investment fees and applicable investment taxes.

How the calculation works

Both paths start with the same capital: whichever option requires more upfront cash. The other path invests the difference. Each month, the less expensive path invests the selected share of its savings at the assumed return; the remainder is consumed. Initial capital differences are invested in full. Contributions occur at month-end.

Buying includes mortgage principal and interest, property tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, incremental utilities, and any modeled mortgage insurance. Principal reduces the loan balance; it is not counted twice as an expense and lost wealth.

At each exit date, buying equals home value minus sale costs and remaining debt, plus investments. Renting equals investments plus the refundable deposit. Buying also recovers the modeled escrow balance and unused prepaid insurance. Points and credits change cash required, not the entered mortgage rate; compare actual paired lender quotes. No separate opportunity-cost charge is added.

Growth compounds monthly from annual assumptions. The maintenance allowance is treated as spending, not saved cash. Known repairs are paid upfront. Taxes, insurance, HOA, maintenance, and utilities grow at the expense-growth rate. For conventional loans, mortgage insurance uses the original loan amount and stops at 80% of the original purchase price; actual cancellation rules vary. VA purchase loans have no monthly mortgage insurance. Their funding fee is calculated on the base loan (price minus down payment): below 5% down, 2.15% first use or 3.3% subsequent use; 5% to under 10%, 1.5%; 10% or more, 1.25%; exempt borrowers, zero. Financing this fee increases the loan balance and interest; paying it at closing increases upfront cash. Other closing costs remain paid upfront. VA rules verified October 25, 2026.

No refinancing, tax effects, investment fees, investment taxes, home-sale taxes, or reinvestment after a move are modeled. Investment return should reflect the return you expect to retain after applicable costs. The historical charts are context, not forecasts.

Sources, data coverage & refresh policy

Market data is a downloaded snapshot retrieved October 25, 2026. This version does not refresh automatically. Each chart shows its latest actual observation; no future observations are inserted. Historical values may be revised by their publishers.

Markets are metros with both home-value and rent history in the downloaded Zillow files, plus the national series. The location selector does not accept ZIP codes in this version. Missing observations are omitted and never replaced with zero. Taxes, insurance, HOA, and maintenance are generic editable allowances, not automatically sourced local costs.

Home-value comparisons use common observation dates. The price/rent chart begins when both series are available, even when all-history is selected. Tooltips and the data table expose the observations used. FHFA is a candidate for an additional long-history comparison; it is not included in this version.

Tax considerations

The comparison excludes personalized tax benefits and liabilities. Before deciding, review:

  • Whether itemizing provides an incremental benefit over your standard deduction.
  • Mortgage-interest and state/local-tax deduction eligibility and limits.
  • Home-sale gain exclusions, ownership and use requirements, and state treatment.
  • Local exemptions, reassessment after purchase, and assistance-program eligibility.

◇

Your personal determinants

A financial advantage is only one part of a housing decision. Consider individually:

  • How certain are you about where you will live and work?
  • Would the home suit changing household or family needs?
  • Are you willing to manage repairs, upkeep, and the selling process?
  • After closing, would you have adequate accessible reserves?
  • How much do stability, flexibility, and control of the property matter to you?

These factors are not scored, monetized, or requested as inputs.
